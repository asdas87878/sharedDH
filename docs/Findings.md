## The short of it
1. **Roblox keeps a new game from under-16s until it passes a gate.** Our players are children. No plan of ours
   carried this. (checked)
2. **Ride a Dino is a superb first hour and a weak second week.** 93% of everyone who joined tamed a dino. A
   player comes back about three times in all. Copy the first hour closely; the long game is not proven.
3. **Its first three minutes are a script,** one line on screen at a time, and it teaches the whole loop. Ours
   teaches only the hop.
4. **The Index is the active way to the stat.** We planned it for last. It is the answer to "progress comes from
   standing still".
5. **Its zones are open.** Anyone who can hop can ride to the far end and look. Creators do it on day one and
   call it "FOMO". It is the game's trailer.
6. **Nothing in it waits for tomorrow.** This is where a newcomer could be better, not just later.
7. **A plot is an open pen in every trending game.** Nothing a player owns is shown behind glass or water.
8. **One "simulator lighting" recipe runs through five tutorials.** Its warm sun colour is the most backed dial,
   and we have never tried it.
9. **Our look has only ever been judged at the top quality level.** On a weak phone there are no shadows, no
   bloom and no glow.
10. **On a phone you pay for draw calls, not parts.** Glass, water, particles, beams and labels are the cost.
11. **The name "Ride a Fish" is already on Roblox,** and our reference is at 3% of its peak.
12. **Several things we built on were misread** (the cap of seven, what size is worth, the night timer). The
    table is further down.

## Rank 1: decide first
**Agreed by the user on 2026-11-16** ("go ahead with the rank 1 proposals"), all but the name. They are in the
plan's order now: [the refinement](Refinement.md). "Rank 1" means decided first, not built first: the user's
word the same day is that the look comes first (the hub's main assets, the UI, the lighting), then these.

### 1. Roblox's gate on new games (checked)
**What "the gate" is.** Roblox will not show a new game to anyone under 16 until the game has passed a check.
Our players are mostly under 16. So until it passes, the people we made it for can't find it or join it.

- A new game is shown only to age-checked players of 16 and over.
- It opens to everyone after **250 plays by "highly engaged" age-checked players within 60 days.** Roblox judges
  "engaged" by account age, play history and spending, to rule out bots.
- **Or the maker pays 50,000 Robux for a review within 48 hours.** This is the route the user has chosen: about
  500 dollars.
- The account that owns the game (or the group's owner) must pass Roblox's ID or face check and have two-step
  sign-in on. **To be checked by the user.**
- There is a second, small fee: 1,000 Robux to publish for all ages. It is waived for an account whose Plus or
  Premium subscription is two months old.

**What "refundable" means, read from Roblox's pages on 2026-11-16:**
- The 50,000 can be asked back after 90 days, if the game is in good standing and keeps 25 highly engaged
  players. If it has fewer, the fee stays in to keep the game open to under-16s.
- The 1,000 comes back if the game keeps 25 highly engaged players for 60 days.
- Neither comes back if the game is taken down for breaking the rules.
- **It comes back as Robux, not dollars.** Bought Robux can't be cashed out; only Robux earned in a game can.
  So the 500 dollars is spent either way. What a refund gives is 50,000 Robux to use on Roblox.

**The budget (the user, 2026-11-16):** about 500 dollars for this, and 100 to 150 or more on adverts. The aim is
to break even. What that takes in sales: [money](design/money.md).
- What developers report this month: about 80 dollars of adverts for 49 of the 250. Adults who pass the age
  check tend to skip this kind of game on sight. (Reddit, not checked)
- **What it means:** the first people who must like our game are over 16. The icon, the title and the first
  minute have to work on them. Or the fee is paid.
- Source: Roblox's own page, and [Ride a Dino](reference/ride-a-dino-deep.md), section 6, point 1.

### 2. The name, and the clock
- **A game titled "[UPD] Ride a Fish" exists:** 793,000 visits, 18 playing. A different loop (eggs and faster
  fish). (not checked)
- **Our reference has faded:** a peak of about 110,000 players around 25 October, about 3,000 to 4,000 on an
  ordinary day now.
- Every game of this kind peaks within a month or two of release, then falls. A verb's window looks like weeks.
- **Fish are crowded, riding is not.** Net a Fish, Harpoon a Fish, Fisch and others are all about catching.
  None is about riding. The title and the icon should say "ride".
- Source: [creators and trending](reference/creators-and-trending.md), sections 4 to 6.

### 3. Open zones: keep them or close them
- In the reference a too-strong dino gives about two seconds, and every landing refills the ring. So anyone can
  hop through all ten zones and look. They can't keep anything from there.
- Creators set this as their first aim and come back saying they need to get stronger.
- Ours works the same way today. [The plan](Refinement.md)'s question 10 asks whether to stop it, and I had said
  yes. **I take that back until both are tried.** Stopping it removes the trailer.
- Source: [Ride a Dino](reference/ride-a-dino-deep.md), section 6, point 4.

### 4. The Index comes forward
- It pays the stat for good: for a first catch of each kind, for a zone's set, for sizes and colours.
- A new player's first catch doubles their stat. A late free player's claims tripled his.
- A paying creator stuck at the last zone was moved on by "Claim All", not by the billion he had bought.
- The red "new!" on a tag is its hook inside the run. We already show tags.
- Ours is in the plan's third wave. I would move it beside the bought trainer.

### 5. The first minutes become a script
What theirs does, in order (seen in three videos of new accounts):
1. A cutscene puts you on the biggest animal before you own anything.
2. One slow, easy animal stands apart from the herd. It can't be missed.
3. The button is boxed and arrowed at each press.
4. The HUD arrives piece by piece. One line of text says what to do next, always.
5. The first failure names its cure: "STOMPED! Stamina 20 / 60 needed".
6. A first purchase at 300 coins and a second at 100, both before minute three.

A dino is tamed 25 seconds after the first prompt. Ours: "three small helps", and nothing to buy.

### 6. Something that waits for tomorrow
- Theirs has no daily goal, no streak, nothing half built. Its last zone just repeats.
- Developers' advice for exactly these numbers: a goal the player can see and can't finish today.
- **The genre's own return hook is a rare arrival on a clock.** Five minutes is what the two biggest use.
  Players build alarms round it. Ours could be a rare shoal.
- Our aquarium is a place to put a goal that lasts.

### 7. Show the fish in the open
- Four trending games, one plot: a low fence of posts and two rails, a gate gap, a small slab sign, the owner's
  face and name in the air. No roof, no glass, no water.
- Ours puts the fish behind glass and tinted water. The pictures the study judged were of the second pass; the
  deep slim tank came after. The point stands for both.
- Fish need water, so ours may still be right. **It is a test, not a ruling:** one plot five ways
  ([buildings](reference/look-assets.md), test 3).

## Rank 3: test in Studio
One change at a time, before and after from the same spot. Needs Studio, so one chat at a time.

### Lighting
Full list of 15: [lighting](reference/look-lighting.md), section 5. The first six:

| # | Test | Ours now | Try |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quality levels.** Nothing else changed | Judged at the top only | Levels 21, 4 and 1 (Test > Device Simulator > Performance) |
| 2 | **A warm sun** | ColorShift_Top not set | 215, 190, 135 |
| 3 | **The shared recipe, whole** | Our own numbers | See below |
| 4 | **Soft or Realistic** | Realistic | Soft, with PrioritizeLightingQuality on. Set by hand in Edit; a script can't |
| 5 | **A lilac shade** | Ambient 118, 128, 138 | 200, 160, 225, then halfway |
| 8 | **Shadow edge** | ShadowSoftness 1, the only 1 in any source | 0.2 |

**The recipe** (five videos; one panel checked): Soft lighting. Brightness 2 to 2.5. Ambient 200, 160, 225.
ColorShift_Top 215, 190, 135. Sky tint 0.3 to 0.4. ShadowSoftness 0.2. Contrast 0.1, Saturation 0.15, white
tint. No bloom. A fog of 200, 170, 250 out to 2,500 studs in place of the Atmosphere. Clock 14, latitude 40.

In words: a pale lilac light in the shade, a warm yellow on faces toward the sun. Ours has neither: light and
shade differ only in brightness. That may be the "washed out" the user keeps reporting. (a guess)

**What a weak phone does not draw:** shadows (under level 4), bloom (menu levels 1 and 2), Neon's glow, sun
rays. Level 1 draws 500 studs. So a look should stand on part colours, Ambient, ColorShift_Top, Brightness and
fog. The recipe is built of exactly those.

**What we already do right:** the sky tint of 0.35, lands told apart by hue and not darkness, the fog tied to
the draw distance, `CastShadow` off on decoration.

### Buildings
Full list of 17: [buildings](reference/look-assets.md), section 5. The first six:

| # | Test | What it settles |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **One plot five ways,** from the glass tank to an open pen | Whether a Rare fish can be told from the middle of the hub |
| 1 | **Studs by material, three ways,** on one stall and one tank base | Where studs go, and how they are laid |
| 2 | **Where the hub's draw calls go:** hide glass and water, labels, lights, stud pictures in turn | What to cut for phones |
| 7 | **The way to the plot:** a trail, an arrow, a marker with metres | Which one a new player follows |
| 5 | **One stall three times against three one-offs** | Whether every shop needs its own shape |
| 15 | **The palette:** stations in pastel, and in brown with two loud stripes | Which belongs on our floor |

**The rules that changed** (already in [the building brief](style/building-brief.md)):
- **Studs go by material, not by facing.** Ground, stone, leaves, cloth and counters are studded on every face
  that shows. Signs, eyes, glass and the reference's timber are plain. (checked)
- **One stall, recoloured, is the habit** for shops. Only machines get their own shape.
- **Stalls are small:** about two players tall. The creatures and the words are what is big.
- **Two or three loud flat colours fill a hub.** Our stations are pastel.
- **Words on the floor,** very large. A trail of chevrons to the plot.
- **A giant creature on a stage by the spawn,** in all four games.

**The two schools of studs disagree, and we should know which we are in.** The trending games stud every showing
face. Builders on Reddit call that "stud slop" and say tops only, whole-stud sizes, square to the grid. The same
threads admit the loud version is what works on children. What both sides attack is the lazy version: cut studs,
thin pieces, plain boxes. Fit the stud to the piece and build thick.

## Rank 4: keep in mind

### Phones
- **A budget, from the developer Roblox asked to write it:** about 500 draw calls and 500,000 triangles in view,
  labels under about 150 draw calls, under 1.3 GB of memory.
- **Plain parts of one material draw together,** whatever their colour or size. Our parts are fine.
- **What never batches:** see-through parts, particle emitters, beams, floating labels. One draw call each.
- **Set `MaxDistance` on every floating label.**
- **Never tween on the server.** Bob and spin on the client.
- **Decoration:** anchored, no collide, no touch, no query, no shadow on small pieces.
- **Thin plates flicker on phones** that don't on a PC. One report needed 0.1 studs clear. Our gate is 0.05.
- **Don't read the player's quality setting** to steer effects. Roblox means to remove it. Our water does.
- Nobody has run the game on a phone. It is still the biggest unknown.

### Money
- **Put each offer on the thing it skips,** with both prices side by side.
- **A first price so small it is not a decision.** Theirs is 3 Robux.
- **A ladder where each step replaces the last,** shown only as your next step.
- **Nothing asks for Robux unasked.** It is always on screen and never in the way.
- **What is bought is the ride back, not the win.** A shop that sells the end of a wait only works while the wait
  is in the way of something loved. Build the thing loved first.
- **The top step must land the buyer somewhere.** Theirs stops just short of the last zone, and all three
  creators who got there felt tricked.
- **A paid prize must not wear a grander label than the thing is.** Three creators said "scam" in their first
  session.
- **The biggest games sell the fewest things:** two passes each, x2 money and x2 of the trained stat, 99 to 299.
- **It earns below its size:** about 90th for players, 312th for earnings.
- **Roblox discounts our prices for its subscribers** by a tenth or a fifth, in our own panels.

### The genre
- **One loop in all of them:** a short act of skill reaches a zone, the zone rolls a creature, it earns on your
  plot, money and one trained stat reach the next zone.
- **One trained stat. Keep it one.** Developers name this as the leader's real strength.
- **The title is the instruction.** A verb and a thing.
- **Small servers:** 5 to 8. Ours is 8.
- **It wins on the first ten seconds,** a number that moves every minute, and a reason to return.
- **Roblox promotes on what happens after the click:** session length, next-day return, days 8 to 28.
- **The same few YouTubers carry each one.** One video took a friend's game from 50 to 1,500 players in a day.
- **Do not copy the video feed** one leader used. Roblox is said to have banned it.
- **A game very like ours was reviewed on Reddit ten days ago.** Its faults are a free checklist: a name that
  hid that riding was the fun, a slow loading screen, no icons in the shop, a separate step to place an animal
  after riding it home, a tutorial that ended on the most confusing machine.

### Why the reference's parts exist
Copied without knowing why, a thing is only its cost. The full table is in
[Ride a Dino](reference/ride-a-dino-deep.md), section 6.
- **The machine:** it paces the zones, it is where the cheapest offer is shown, and it is a public seat. Early
  sits are twenty seconds. Late ones are an hour and a half, and everybody names those as the problem.
- **Carrying the catch home:** a parade, and it shows newcomers what exists.
- **The earners' board:** a number absurdly above yours, passed on the way to every run.
- **The neighbour's pen:** it is the advert, the goal and the price list.
- **Three free revives:** teaching the button before charging for it.

### Where it comes from
- The ride is **Rodeo Stampede**, a phone game of 2016. The hub is **Steal An Egg**'s.
- The parent game had slow motion in the leap, obstacles, and animals with tempers. Ride a Dino took out the
  danger and put in a stat.
- Its own Endless mode, added later, has every missing thing. Worth ten minutes of the user's time to play
  Rodeo Stampede.

## What I am not sure of
- **Why it got big.** The study leans to: a proven ride, a superb first hour, a wave of YouTubers, and nothing
  that holds a second week. It is sure of the shape and not the weights.
- **Whether the hop is what they stay for.** Creators shout about it in the first minute. By minute ten they
  talk about the pen's total. Only the user can tell which it is, by playing.
- **Whether a run should be able to fail hard.** Theirs can't. Ours drowns you. Ours may be the better game; it
  is not the same feeling.
- **How the reference is built** (parts or meshes) and **how it is lit.** No source shows either.
- **What anything sounds like.** Nineteen videos, and nothing learned about sound.
- **The creators study's Reddit counts.** The archive was down when I tried to recount.
