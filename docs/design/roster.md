# Creature roster

**Picked 2026-11-09: variant C, "a giant in every zone", reworked the same day so rarity slides with the zones** (the user's
correction: in Ride a Dino a zone doesn't hold one of each rarity; Commons only exist early, and the mix moves up the further you
go), again so a zone's rare spawns always fit it and the coolest Legendaries only open up later, and again so creatures range
one or two zones ahead of home, with bigger Common and Uncommon pools at the start. 80 creatures. The same list one creature a
line, with a short look, for image prompts: [roster list](roster-list.md).

The user's brief: at or above Ride a Dino's scale. Start with small, basic, nice-looking fish (one striped, one plain), move on to
the cooler fish of the sea, then the huge, insane-looking prehistoric ones, then our own mythical ones for the late, late game
(demon fish, half-skeleton fish).

## How Ride a Dino does it
- **"50+ species"** across **"9 wild eras"**, says the official game page
  ([roblox.com/games/93523763376122](https://www.roblox.com/games/93523763376122)); the user's recording shows 10 zones. No full
  list is published anywhere.
- **Each species has one rarity; each zone has its own species; so each zone has its own mix,** and the mix slides up the further
  you go. Measured from the recording (the "Rarity and spawns" section of the [breakdown](../reference/ride-a-dino.md)): the
  first zone is Commons and Uncommons; Commons last about 200 m into the second zone and then never appear again; the second zone
  is mostly Uncommon with Rares, Epics now and then, and a Legendary rarely.
- **Required stamina** rises with rarity and about 5 times per zone; a guide says the last zone's top dinos need millions.
- **Secret and Eternal** spawns are zone events announced to the whole server.

## Our roster

### Where each creature spawns
The user's rules: a creature can swim one or two zones ahead of its home, so each zone mixes its own creatures with some from the
zones before it, for far more variety; a rare spawn in an early zone still has to make sense there (a huge turtle in the
Shallows); and the coolest Legendaries can't turn up early by luck.
- **A spawn picks its rarity from the zone's mix (above), then a creature of that rarity that can be there,** weighted by how far
  it is from home: full weight at home, about a third one zone on, about a tenth two zones on.
- **Commons only reach the start of the Coral Reef,** as the recording shows (Plains Commons up to about 220 m into the Forest).
  The recording only covers two zones, so the one-or-two-zone range for everything else comes from the user's play.
- **Never backwards:** a creature never turns up before its home zone (except the extremely rare strays, below). So the
  Shallows' 1 in 170 Epic is a reef shark and its 1 in 500 Legendary is Archelon, never a Megalodon.
- **Unless it wouldn't fit:** a few stay home, like Steller's Sea Cow, which lived in kelp forests and ate kelp.
- **Every rarity a zone spawns has creatures to fill it,** from home or from the zones just behind (the Open Blue's few
  Uncommons are the Barracuda, Garibaldi and Sea Lion).

### Strays: the touch of luck
The user wanted a touch of an extremely rare chance that something from outside a zone's pool turns up early. Strays are the one
exception to the rules above (draft numbers, to tune):
- **How rare:** a stray Legendary about 1 in 10,000 spawns, in any zone: twenty times rarer than the Shallows' own Legendary (1 in
  500). A stray Mythic (the user's addition) ten times rarer again, about 1 in 100,000.
- **Only from close by:** from the next one or two zones, as if it wandered off course. A Dunkleosteus or a Steller's Sea Cow in
  the Shallows, never a Megalodon; the earliest a Megalodon can stray is the Twilight zone. The first Mythic that can stray is the
  Blue Whale, into the Kelp Forest or the Shipwrecks; the Hellmaw can stray as far up as the Vents. Never a Secret.
- **It's clearly lost:** a "STRAY" tag and a faint glowing trail, and the whole server hears about it ("A stray Dunkleosteus has
  wandered into the Shallows!"), so everyone nearby rushes over.
- **Catchable, just:** taming a stray Legendary takes the stamina of the zone it strayed into, as hard as that zone's own
  Legendary, so the lucky player has a real shot; a stray Mythic takes twice that. It keeps a "Stray" mark in the collection book:
  something to show off.
- **One stray per server at a time.**

### Our target, still
- **Sizes and mutations** (our own names, to pick later) turn 80 into about 2,000 entries in the collection book, almost free:
  sizes are scale, mutations are a colour swap ([creature method](../style/creature-method.md), step 2).
- **Launch with zones 1 to 4** (37 creatures), then a zone per update, the way this genre grows.
- **"Fish" means anything that swims**, the way Ride a Dino has mammoths and gorillas: sharks, whales, turtles, squid, sea
  scorpions and sea reptiles all count.

### Signature looks (the giants and the myths)
- **Archelon:** a turtle the size of a car, beaked head, ridged shell. **Grave Turtle** is it again, undead, in the Bone Graveyard.
- **Helicoprion:** a shark-like fish with a spiral buzz-saw of teeth in its lower jaw.
- **Dunkleosteus:** an armoured head of bone plates, a jaw of bony blades instead of teeth.
- **Steller's Sea Cow:** a gentle giant as long as a bus, bark-like bumpy skin, a small head, a whale's tail; it grazed on kelp
  and was hunted out in 1768.
- **Xiphactinus:** a huge bulldog face, underbite full of fangs.
- **Elasmosaurus:** a neck longer than the rest of its body.
- **Liopleurodon** and **Kronosaurus:** crocodile heads on whale bodies, four giant flippers (Kronosaurus bigger and scarred).
- **Shonisaurus:** a dolphin-shaped ichthyosaur the size of a whale.
- **Mosasaurus:** a giant sea lizard, double row of teeth, a shark-like tail fluke.
- **Livyatan:** a sperm whale with teeth like tusks. **Basilosaurus:** a whale as long and thin as a sea serpent.
- **Anomalocaris:** an alien-looking hunter with two spiny grabbing arms and eyes on stalks.
- **Megalodon:** the biggest jaw in the game, scarred. **Molten Megalodon:** the same, cracked with glowing lava from the vents.
  **Halfbone Megalodon** (the user's half-skeleton fish): front half flesh, back half bare skeleton.
- **Blue Whale:** the biggest animal ever. **Kraken:** a giant octopus with glowing suckers. **Lantern Leviathan:** a giant
  deep-sea fish with a lure like a lighthouse.
- **Bone Leviathan:** a whole serpent skeleton, swimming. **Hellmaw** (the user's demon fish): horns, lava cracks between its
  scales, ember eyes, burning fins. **Abyssal Watcher:** a body covered in eyes.
- **Secrets:** Pearl Serpent (pearl-white, a pearl in its brow), Coral Dragon (grown out of coral), Kelp Wyrm (wrapped in kelp),
  Galleon Whale (a sunken ship's hull grown onto its back), Storm Serpent (crackling with lightning), Aurora Jelly (a giant
  jellyfish glowing like the northern lights), Void Whale (a whale-shaped hole full of stars), Ember Serpent (fire from the
  vents), Phantom Whale (see-through), The Devourer (the last one: mostly mouth).

## Starter pair (zone 1's first two Commons)
Small, basic, nice-looking: one striped, one plain. Options:
1. **Clownfish** (orange with white bands) + **Yellow Tang** (all lemon yellow): warm and bright; the clownfish is the fish every
   kid knows.
2. **Sergeant Major** (pale yellow with bold black bars) + **Blue Chromis** (vivid electric blue): clean, and the two colours pop
   against white sand.
3. **Banded Butterflyfish** (white with black bands) + **Red Snapper** (coral red): the plainest "fish" shapes.
